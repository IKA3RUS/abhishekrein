<Section id="context">

**more** is one of India's largest supermarket brands with 800 storefronts and
₹6000 crore in annual revenue. In 2022, **more** launched a grocery delivery
app, but quick-commerce players had established a stronghold on the urban
markets with hyperlocal speeds. Behavioural research revealed the real
opportunity to us: **more**'s customers valued in-store shopping, not delivery
speed. What if we met them where they already were - at our supermarkets?

Research identified key friction points: crowded aisles, finding products, and
checkout queues. Equally important was elevating the **more+** membership which
had insane adoption offline, with it's guaranteed 5% cashback, but wasn't a
highlight of the digital experience. This study explores how we fundamentally
shifted and repositioned **more**'s digital strategy from standalone delivery to
an holistic system that enhanced the in-store experience.

## visualizing <br /> savings with gamification

Everyone starts at level (_LVL_) 0 with a personalized avatar. Reaching level 1
means recovering your membership fee through savings. The few early levels
follow a linear [reward shaping](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2002.10904) ramp. Each level after follows an
_exponential difficulty curve based on real (& realtime) spending data_.

### TOTP for everyone

We designed **secure pin** as a Supermarket Sense feature that automatically
appears when customers enter a **more** supermarket. The secure pin enables
instant verification for **more+** point and coupon redemptions, replacing slow,
unreliable SMS-based OTPs.

**secure pin** is always ready, resets every 2 minutes, and even works with no
network - for all locations with janky internet.

### enabling self checkout

**self shop** is an experimental, forward-thinking, Supermarket Sense
feature that enables a self-shopping experience for **more**’s flagship
supermarkets.

It features a complete self-checkout experience without requiring additional
infrastructure, while also increasing familiarity and training users for
**more**’s grocery delivery platform.

### localized supermarket offers

**more** used to rely on SMS and WhatsApp for all offer communication for its
physical supermarkets. We created an in-app feed to communicate all active
campaigns from the customer's selected supermarket.

## price talkers

**more**'s app and the shelves had to speak the same language. We redesigned our
price talker range to bridge them. The new layouts prominently display **more+**
cashbacks, without taking away attention from the actual product prices.

The regular-sized regular type talkers are optimized for **more**'s standard
shelf insert heights - fitting upto 17 per A4 sized sheet compared to the
previous 8. A roughly 55% paper and monetary savings. Yay environment!

# reflections

Repositioning a delivery app around the supermarket experience sounds clean in
hindsight, but the direction wasn't always obvious. **more** had invested in
competing in the same market as quick-commerce, and stepping back from that
domain required the research to do real unlearning. Our customers weren't
looking for speed. They were already at the store and were under-served there.

> The aspects of things that are most important for us are hidden because of their simplicity and familiarity. One is unable to notice something because it is always before one's eyes.
>
> --- Ludwig Wittgenstein, Philosophical Investigations §129

The tension that stayed with us throughout was success attribution. When success
lives partly in the app and partly in a physical aisle, it's hard to draw a
clean line. We didn't fully resolve this. Some of this work succeeds in ways
that won't show up in app metrics, and we've learnt to accept that.

Supermarket Sense was designed to outlast this project. Naming it as a series
rather than a set of features gave future work a home with its foundations
already built. The TOTP (secure pin) and self-checkout (self shop) features only
began to explore what's possible when the app knows you're physically inside a
supermarket. As **more** scales and its flagship supermarkets mature, that
context-awareness becomes a real differentiator, something quick-commerce
players structurally can't replicate because they don't have the supermarkets.
The problem we tried to solve here was not create a better app. The opportunity
here was the creation of a different relationship between a supermarket and the
customer already inside.
